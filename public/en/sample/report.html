What to fix – prioritised list

The site is quick to load on desktop and scores well for accessibility and basic SEO, but it has several gaps in security and search visibility. The most important fix is to make the http:// and non-www versions of the site redirect to one secure address. Right now they load as separate copies, which weakens security and can split Google's view of the site.

  1. Priority: high Effort: 30 min · Impact: Security and trust for every visitor

    Redirect http:// to https://

    Visitors who type or follow an old http:// link get an unencrypted copy of the site instead of being sent to the secure one. This is a security risk and can hurt trust and Google ranking.

    How: In the nginx configuration (or at your hosting provider), add a permanent 301 redirect from http:// to https:// for all pages. Then test http://www.krakow.pl in a browser to confirm it lands on https://.

  2. Priority: high Effort: 30 min · Impact: Avoids split Google ranking

    Pick one address: www or non-www

    Both www.krakow.pl and krakow.pl load as separate sites without redirecting to each other. Google may treat them as duplicates and split ranking between them.

    How: Choose one version (www.krakow.pl is what you use now) and add a 301 redirect from the other to it in nginx. Also add a canonical link tag on every page pointing to the chosen address.

  3. Priority: high Effort: 1-2 h · Impact: Stronger protection for visitors

    Add security headers

    None of the standard browser protection headers are set. This leaves visitors more exposed to clickjacking and content-injection attacks, and it contributes to the lower Best Practices score of 77.

    How: Add these in nginx: Strict-Transport-Security (once the https redirect works), X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN, Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin and a Permissions-Policy. Add a Content-Security-Policy last and test it carefully, because it can break embedded content if it is too strict.

  4. Priority: high Effort: 15 min · Impact: Prevents a site-wide browser warning

    Renew the security certificate in time

    The HTTPS certificate expires in 33 days. If it lapses, browsers will show a scary warning and most visitors will leave.

    How: Confirm that automatic renewal (for example Let's Encrypt/certbot) is working, or renew the certificate manually now. Set a calendar reminder or uptime monitoring that alerts at least 14 days before expiry.

  5. Priority: medium Effort: 1-2 h · Impact: Faster, more complete Google indexing

    Fix the missing sitemap

    The sitemap address at /sitemap.xml returns a 404 error, so Google has no official list of pages to crawl. New or deep pages may be found more slowly.

    How: Generate a sitemap.xml with your CMS or a sitemap tool and publish it at the root. Add its address to robots.txt (Sitemap: https://www.krakow.pl/sitemap.xml) and submit it in Google Search Console.

  6. Priority: medium Effort: 30 min · Impact: Clearer search results, more clicks

    Add a page heading and meta description

    The home page has no H1 heading and an empty meta description. Google then picks its own snippet for search results, and the page's main topic is less clear.

    How: Add one H1 heading to the home page that states what the site is. Write a meta description of about 150 characters in Polish that summarises the site and invites a click.

  7. Priority: medium Effort: 15 min · Impact: Cleaner, more clickable search listing

    Clean up the page title

    The title repeats itself ("Oficjalny serwis miejski - Oficjalny serwis miejski - Magiczny Kraków") and is 69 characters long, so Google may cut it off in results.

    How: Remove the repeated phrase and aim for 60 characters or fewer, for example "Magiczny Kraków - Oficjalny serwis miejski". Put the most important words first.

  8. Priority: medium Effort: dev needed · Impact: Faster mobile experience

    Speed up the main content on mobile

    On mobile, the largest visible element takes about 5.4 seconds to appear, which is slow. Google recommends under 2.5 seconds, and slow pages lose visitors.

    How: Have your developer find the largest element at the top of the home page, usually a big image. Compress it, serve it in a modern format such as WebP, and do not lazy-load it. Also reduce server response time, which Lighthouse says could save roughly 0.5 seconds.

  9. Priority: medium Effort: dev needed · Impact: More stable, less frustrating pages

    Stop the desktop layout from jumping

    On desktop, content shifts around while the page loads (layout shift score 0.40, where 0.1 or less is good). This causes accidental clicks and feels unpolished.

    How: Ask your developer to set fixed width and height on images, banners and embedded widgets so space is reserved before they load. Check any cookie banner or pop-up that appears after the page loads.

  10. Priority: low Effort: 1 h · Impact: Better shared links and branding

    Add social sharing tags and a favicon

    There are no Open Graph or Twitter card tags, so shared links on Facebook, LinkedIn or X show up plain and unattractive. No favicon is detected either, so the browser tab has no icon.

    How: Add og:title, og:description and og:image tags to the page head, plus a twitter:card tag. Upload a favicon (a small square logo) and link it in the head.

  11. Priority: low Effort: 1-2 h · Impact: Accessibility and legal compliance

    Add alt text and fix the pop-up label

    12 of 46 images have no alt text, and a dialog pop-up has no accessible name. Screen reader users cannot understand these, and it is a legal accessibility concern for a public site.

    How: Add short descriptive alt text to the 12 images (or an empty alt="" for purely decorative ones). Give the dialog an aria-label or aria-labelledby that describes it.

  12. Priority: low Effort: dev needed · Impact: Privacy compliance and better score

    Review third-party cookies and browser console issues

    Lighthouse flags third-party cookies and browser-reported issues, which lower the Best Practices score to 77. Third-party cookies can also raise privacy-consent concerns.

    How: Have your developer open Chrome DevTools, check the Issues tab and see which embedded services set cookies. Remove ones you do not need and make sure the rest are covered by your cookie consent banner.
