In-depth website audit: kamilobstawski.com

https://kamilobstawski.com/pl/ · Report generated 06/11/2026

Scope: 30 pages (SEO of each), 73 links, 4 key pages inspected on a phone (errors, forms, cookie banner), customer journeys, Lighthouse mobile+desktop, accessibility on 4 pages.

Summary

Your site is in very good shape: desktop scores 100 across the board, mobile accessibility, best practices and SEO are 100, and the page loads in under a second. The one thing worth fixing first is the contact form, because it is how clients reach you. It has one unlabelled field, no spam protection and no privacy notice. The mobile slowness (performance 86, TBT 555 ms) comes from a single script, consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js. A broken download on one blog post and overly long titles on 21 pages are quick wins. Security headers, HTTPS, compression and the 404 page are already fine.

Action plan – most important first

First step: Open /pl/kontakt/ and add a visible label to the one unlabelled field, then add a honeypot and a short privacy notice to the form.

  1. high Effort: 1–2 h · Impact: More usable form, less junk mail, GDPR-ready

    Fix the contact form: label, spam guard and privacy notice

    On /pl/kontakt/ 1 of 4 fields has no label, so screen-reader users and anyone who has started typing can't tell what it is. The form also has no spam protection and no data-processing notice, although it collects e-mail addresses.

    How: Find the field without a label in the contact template and give it a real label element tied to the input id. Add a hidden honeypot field, or Cloudflare Turnstile, and reject submissions where the honeypot is filled in. Add one line under the submit button linking to your privacy policy, or a required consent checkbox. If you have no privacy policy page yet, create a short one.

  2. medium Effort: developer, 2–4 h · Impact: Faster response to taps on phones, better Core Web Vitals

    Reduce main-thread work from consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js

    On mobile, the page is busy for 555 ms of blocking time (Google wants 200–300 ms or less), and the mobile score is 86 against 100 on desktop. The heaviest file is /static/js/consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js, which occupies the CPU for 4334 ms in the test.

    How: No scripts block rendering, so adding defer or async will not help. Have your developer profile consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js in Chrome DevTools (Performance tab, 4x CPU throttling). Start heavy work, such as animations or the 3 canvas/WebGL elements, after the page has loaded or when the browser is idle, and split long tasks (19 of them, 1.7 s in total). The lab test may overstate the WebGL cost, so confirm with real-user data in Google Search Console (Core Web Vitals report).

    Ready-made fix
    window.addEventListener('load', () => {
  const start = () => initHeavyAnimations();
  if ('requestIdleCallback' in window) {
    requestIdleCallback(start, { timeout: 2000 });
  } else {
    setTimeout(start, 200);
  }
});

    How you will know: The re-check shows mobile TBT under 300 ms and a mobile performance score of 90 or more.

    Covers findings: perf.tbt.mobile perf.score.mobile perf.heavy-script@consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js

  3. medium Effort: 15 min · Impact: No dead links in blog content, better reader trust

    Repair the broken download link in the blog post

    The file /static/downloads/laaj-3-runs.zip returns a 404 error. The link is on /pl/blog/decyzje-ktorych-nikt-nie-podjal/ and /en/blog/decyzje-ktorych-nikt-nie-podjal/, so readers hit a dead end.

    How: Upload the file to /static/downloads/ on the server under that exact name, or edit both posts to point to the file's current location. If the file no longer exists, remove the link and the sentence around it. Check that the same file is available for both language versions.

    How you will know: The re-check finds that https://kamilobstawski.com/static/downloads/laaj-3-runs.zip returns HTTP 200, or that no page links to it.

    Covers findings: links.broken@https://kamilobstawski.com/static/downloads/laaj-3-runs.zip

  4. low Effort: 1–2 h · Impact: Cleaner search results, better click-through

    Shorten page titles on blog pages (21 pages)

    21 pages have titles longer than Google's roughly 60 visible characters, up to 133 characters (for example /pl/blog/claude-code-build-eval/ and its /en/ version). Google cuts them off in search results, which can weaken click-through.

    How: In your blog template, find where the page title is built. Stop appending the site name to long post titles, or cap the title at about 60 characters. For posts with very long titles, write a shorter dedicated SEO title field. Start with /pl/blog/, /en/blog/ and the posts above 100 characters.

    How you will know: The re-check finds no page title over about 60 characters.

    How: Find the CSS rule that sets the font size for the home page paragraphs (likely a hero or intro text class) and raise it to at least 16 px. Check the layout on a phone afterwards, as larger text may wrap differently.

  6. low Effort: 1–2 h · Impact: Extra protection against script injection

    Add a Content-Security-Policy header (hardening)

    The site sends no Content-Security-Policy header. This is an optional hardening step that limits the damage if a malicious script is ever injected. Your site has no logins or payments, so the risk is low.

    How: Add the header at your web server or CDN. Start in report-only mode to see what would break, then enforce it once the console shows no violations. Adjust the sources if you load external fonts, analytics or embeds.

Your site through a customer’s eyes

An AI consultant looked at screenshots of the key pages on a phone and a desktop as a first-time visitor. Every remark has evidence (screenshot and quote); technical items are in the other sections.

First impression: A visually distinctive, developer-flavoured personal site. The headline explains the author's method (an AI agent fleet) rather than what a client can buy. A stranger sees no buy-now action, so they can't tell what to do next.

high

The home page never says what you sell, to whom, or what to do next

The H1 describes the owner's working method (agent fleet) rather than a customer outcome. A first-time visitor on a phone can't tell what service they can buy (websites? apps? consulting?), whether it's for them, or what the next step is. The first screen has no button at all.

Evidence: Screenshot 1: H1 'Buduję całe produkty z flotą agentów AI — a o tym, co jest poprawne, decyduję ja.' The subline is 'Kamil Obstawski, full-stack developer. 10 lat w komercji…'. The only links are the inline 'na blogu' and 'opisany w całości'. The decorative '$ until good; do refine; done' line adds nothing a customer can use. Screenshot 2 (desktop) has the same headline and no button.

Suggestion: Rewrite the H1 around the outcome and the audience, e.g. 'Strony i aplikacje webowe dla firm — budowane szybciej dzięki agentom AI, odpowiadam za każdą linijkę'. Add a primary button under the subline, such as 'Opisz swój projekt →' linking to /pl/kontakt/. Add a secondary 'Zobacz cennik' link. Keep the agent-fleet story lower on the page.

high

No primary CTA above the fold; the contact link looks like any other tab

The above-the-fold links are all navigation: the bottom tab bar and two inline text links. Nothing is styled as a call to action. 'kontakt' is one of four equal tabs, in muted grey on the mobile home page. The page offers no contact route that stands out.

Evidence: Screenshot 1: bottom bar '~/ko ~/blog ~/kontakt ~/o mnie', all equally subdued. The home page has no visible button, and the data lists only nav items plus a theme toggle. Screenshot 3: the form is reachable only via the 'kontakt' tab.

Suggestion: Add a filled, high-contrast button in the amber accent colour ('Napisz do mnie' or 'Wyceń projekt'). Make it sticky or repeat it at the end of the first text block. Consider tinting the 'kontakt' tab with the accent colour.

high

Cryptic developer-style labels hide the offer and pricing from non-technical visitors

The navigation and section labels use terminal jargon: '~/ko', '~/blog', '~/kontakt', '~/o mnie', '$ whoami', '$ ls ./blog', '$ curl ./blog/feed.xml', '$ series przepiecie'. Business owners may not understand them. No 'Oferta', 'Usługi' or 'Cennik' item is visible in the navigation, even though the contact page refers to a price table and calculators.

Evidence: Screenshots 1, 3, 4, 5: tab bar '~/ko ~/blog ~/kontakt ~/o mnie' and prompts like '$ whoami'. Screenshot 3 text: 'Tabela cen podaje widełki, a kalkulatory liczą stronę albo aplikację webową w minutę.' The price table and calculators are not reachable from the visible nav. '~/ko' is not an obvious label for 'home'.

Suggestion: Keep the terminal look as decoration, but use plain words for the tabs, such as 'Start', 'Oferta', 'Cennik', 'Blog', 'Kontakt'. Put a 'Cennik / Kalkulator' link in the main nav and on the home page. Replace '$ curl ./blog/feed.xml' with 'Subskrybuj RSS'.

medium

Contact: e-mail address not visible or tappable on the page; form is long and its end is unseen

The contact page shows no visible e-mail address, phone number or hours. The only route is the form. The form opens with 4 fields (name, email, timing chips, project description), and the visitor has to scroll to see the rest and the submit button. The promise 'Odpisuję w ciągu jednego dnia roboczego' is a strong trust element. The copy 'mailem — nie drugim formularzem' sits oddly next to a form.

Evidence: Screenshot 3: labels 'imię>', 'email>', 'na kiedy>' with chips 'jak najszybciej / w tym miesiącu / nie pali się / mam termin', then 'czego potrzebujesz>' cut off at the bottom. The data shows 4 fields, submit 'Wyślij →', no consent checkbox, 0 tel links, 1 mailto. The form journey returned 'warn'.

Suggestion: Show the e-mail address as a tappable mailto link directly under the intro paragraph, and a phone or messenger link if the owner takes calls. Keep the form to name, email and message. The timing chips are a nice touch and can stay as optional. After submit, show a clear confirmation. Reword the intro so it doesn't contradict itself.

medium

Little visible proof for a prospective customer: no portfolio, clients or testimonials

The home page asks for trust without showing proof. The text says client names are under NDA. The case study and the price table are not visible in the screenshots. There is no photo of the owner and no logo, testimonial or screenshot of finished work. The agent-window graphic on desktop is decorative and shows no real output.

Evidence: Screenshot 1: 'nazwy klientów zostają za NDA'; the only link to evidence is 'opisany w całości'. Screenshot 5 (about) is a text wall with no photo. Screenshot 2: the faux terminal windows ('agent · explore', 'agent · write', 'agent · review', '2 edge cases flagged') are not a portfolio. The desktop graphic is also absent on mobile.

Suggestion: Add a 'Wybrane realizacje' strip with 2–3 anonymised results (e.g. 'strona + rezerwacje online dla gabinetu: 3 tygodnie'). Add a photo of Kamil on the About page. Quote any client feedback that can be shared. Link the case study with a button, not an inline text link.

medium

Low-contrast grey text and dense paragraphs hurt mobile reading

The intro text and the blog lead are mid-grey monospace on near-black, which reads weakly. The home page has long, unbroken paragraphs. A floating glowing orb overlaps body text on several pages.

Evidence: Screenshot 1: the grey monospace subline 'Kamil Obstawski, full-stack developer…' is dim against the background, and the orb sits over 'tylko inne' in the body text. Screenshot 5: the orb covers 'prowadzisz firmę' in the About text. Screenshot 3: it overlaps the 'czego potrzebujesz>' field. Screenshot 4: the grey intro 'Trzy serie i case studies…' is a long five-line block. The data reports a minimum paragraph font of 12.5px on home.

Suggestion: Lighten the secondary text (aim for contrast ≥ 7:1) and set body text at 16px or larger. Break the long paragraphs into short blocks with sub-headings. Move the orb out of the reading area, or make it non-overlapping and smaller on mobile, so it never covers text or form fields.

low

The blog leads with a long series post rather than something for buyers

The first blog entry is part 1 of a series about migrating a PHP site. It is interesting to developers, but a prospective client lands on it without context. The page shows a large atmospheric image with no description of what the post means for a customer.

Evidence: Screenshot 4: '$ series przepiecie', '1 część', a large dark image of two pillars, '02 Lis 2026 · 8 min czytania'. The header text mentions 'wejście do zawodu' (career entry advice), which is not aimed at customers.

Suggestion: Add a short customer-oriented line to each post ('Co to znaczy dla Twojej firmy: …'). Put a contact CTA at the end of the blog list and of each post.

What works well – don’t break it

  • Mobile layout is clean and uncluttered. There is no cookie banner or pop-up covering content, the bottom tab bar is easy to reach with a thumb, and the PL/EN and dark/light toggles are clearly visible.
  • The contact page copy sets clear expectations ('Odpisuję w ciągu jednego dnia roboczego') and the 'na kiedy' chips make the form quick to fill in. The About page gives concrete facts (Kraków, remote work, PL/EN, B2B invoice).
