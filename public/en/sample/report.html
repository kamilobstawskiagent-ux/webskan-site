Summary

Your site is in very good shape: desktop scores 100 across the board, mobile accessibility, best practices and SEO are 100, the certificate is healthy and the main security headers are in place. The one thing worth fixing first is the mobile responsiveness problem (Total Blocking Time 644 ms, mobile score 84), caused almost entirely by one script, consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js. There is also a broken download link on one blog post (in both languages) that sends readers to a 404. The remaining items are small tidy-ups for search results and privacy.

Action plan – most important first

First step: Ask your developer to open /static/js/consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js in the Chrome DevTools Performance panel and find which part runs the longest, then start that part after the page has loaded.

  1. high Effort: developer, 2–4 h · Impact: Snappier taps on phones; better Core Web Vitals (INP) and mobile score

    Delay heavy work in consultant.js until after page load

    On mobile, Total Blocking Time is 644 ms (a safe level is 200–300 ms) and the mobile score is 84/100. The script consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js keeps the CPU busy for 4281 ms in the test, with 18 long tasks totalling 1590 ms, so taps may feel laggy on phones.

    How: Profile /static/js/consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js in Chrome DevTools (Performance tab, 4x CPU throttling) and find the functions behind the long tasks. Start heavy animations or set-up work after the load event or when the browser is idle, instead of immediately. The home page has 3 canvas elements, so lab measurement may overstate the real cost of WebGL/canvas drawing: confirm with Search Console field data (Core Web Vitals report) before and after. Note that no render-blocking scripts were found, so do not add defer or async as a fix.

    Ready-made fix
    window.addEventListener('load', () => {
  const start = () => initHeavyAnimations(); // your existing init function
  if ('requestIdleCallback' in window) {
    requestIdleCallback(start, { timeout: 2000 });
  } else {
    setTimeout(start, 200);
  }
});

    How you will know: A re-check should show mobile TBT at or below about 300 ms and a mobile performance score of 90 or more.

    Covers findings: perf.tbt.mobile perf.score.mobile perf.heavy-script@consultant.895c86c0f2ae.js

  2. medium Effort: 15 min · Impact: No dead links for readers; cleaner site quality signals

    Fix the broken download link in the 'decyzje' blog post

    The file /static/downloads/laaj-3-runs.zip returns 404, and it is linked from the blog post 'decyzje-ktorych-nikt-nie-podjal' in both the Polish and English versions. Readers who click it hit a dead end, which looks careless on a portfolio.

    How: Upload the missing laaj-3-runs.zip to the static/downloads folder on the server, or correct the file name if it was renamed. If the file should no longer be offered, remove the link from both /pl/blog/decyzje-ktorych-nikt-nie-podjal/ and /en/blog/decyzje-ktorych-nikt-nie-podjal/. After deploying, open the URL directly to confirm it downloads.

    How you will know: The URL https://kamilobstawski.com/static/downloads/laaj-3-runs.zip returns HTTP 200 (or the links are gone) and no broken links are reported.

    Covers findings: links.broken@https://kamilobstawski.com/static/downloads/laaj-3-runs.zip

  3. medium Effort: 30 min–1 h · Impact: GDPR compliance and more visitor trust

    Add a privacy notice to the contact form

    The form on /pl/kontakt/ collects an e-mail address and a message but has no data-processing notice or privacy policy link. Under GDPR, visitors should be told how their data is used.

    How: Add a short sentence with a link to a privacy policy page under the form's submit button in the contact template. If you do not yet have a privacy policy, publish a brief one stating who receives the data, why, and how to ask for deletion. A checkbox is not required for a simple contact form, a clear notice is usually enough, but check with your legal adviser.

    Ready-made fix
    <p class="form-note">Wysyłając formularz, zgadzasz się na przetwarzanie danych w celu odpowiedzi na wiadomość. <a href="/pl/polityka-prywatnosci/">Polityka prywatności</a></p>

    How you will know: The /pl/kontakt/ page should contain a privacy notice or link near the form, and the 'no consent' warning should disappear.

    Covers findings: ux.form.no_consent@/pl/kontakt/

  4. low Effort: 1–2 h · Impact: Cleaner search results and possibly higher click-through

    Shorten page titles on blog pages to about 60 characters

    21 pages (both /pl/ and /en/ blog pages) have titles of up to 133 characters, for example /pl/blog/claude-code-build-eval/ and /en/blog/claude-code-build-eval/. Google cuts titles at around 60 characters, so search results show truncated text.

    How: In the blog post template, stop appending the site name to long titles, or write a shorter dedicated title field for each post. Put the most important words first and keep the full title in the H1. Start with the longest ones: claude-code-build-eval, spec-przez-wywiad-z-llm, jaki-jezyk-programowania-wybrac.

    Ready-made fix
    <title>Short post title | Kamil Obstawski</title>
(aim for 60 characters or fewer)

    How you will know: A re-check should find no titles over about 60 characters, or far fewer than the current 21.

    Covers findings: seo.title.long

  5. low Effort: developer, 1–2 h · Impact: Extra protection against script injection

    Add a Content-Security-Policy header (hardening)

    The site does not send a Content-Security-Policy header. The site has no logins or payments, so this is a low-priority hardening step that limits the damage if a malicious script were ever injected.

    How: First add the header in report-only mode on your web server (nginx or Apache) or CDN and watch the browser console for blocked resources, then enforce it. Adjust the sources to match the scripts, fonts and images you actually use.

    Ready-made fix
    add_header Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only "default-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; script-src 'self'" always;

    How you will know: The response headers for https://kamilobstawski.com/pl/ should include content-security-policy.

    Covers findings: sec.header.csp

Your site through a customer’s eyes

An AI consultant looked at screenshots of the key pages on a phone and a desktop as a first-time visitor. Every remark has evidence (screenshot and quote); technical items are in the other sections.

First impression: A polished, stylish developer portfolio. A stranger sees a long manifesto about working with AI agents, but not what they can order or what to do next. It feels aimed at other developers rather than at a business owner who needs a website or app.

high

The headline describes the method, not what the visitor can buy

The home headline is about how the owner works (a fleet of AI agents). It never says what a customer gets (websites, web apps, something else) or who it is for. The page reads like a developer's personal manifesto. A business owner can't tell in 5 seconds whether this is for them. The wording about NDAs and 'what is correct, I decide' is aimed at peers.

Evidence: Screenshots 1 and 2: H1 'Buduję całe produkty z flotą agentów AI — a o tym, co jest poprawne, decyduję ja.' The sub-line says 'Kamil Obstawski, full-stack developer. 10 lat w komercji, nazwy klientów zostają za NDA.' The eyebrow reads '$ until good; do refine; done'. No offer, audience or price is stated above the fold.

Suggestion: Put the offer first, for example: 'Strony i aplikacje webowe dla firm — gotowe szybciej, bo buduję je z agentami AI, a odpowiadam za nie nazwiskiem.' Keep the current line as the sub-headline. Replace the '$ until good…' eyebrow with plain language, such as 'Full-stack developer · Kraków · zdalnie'.

high

No primary call to action on the home page

There is no button above the fold on mobile or desktop. The only clickable items in the content are two small inline links, 'na blogu' and 'opisany w całości'. Both send the visitor to reading material, not to contact or pricing. The contact page mentions a price table and calculators, but the home page doesn't link to them. A ready-to-buy visitor has no obvious next step.

Evidence: Screenshot 1: below the H1 there are only orange inline links 'na blogu' and 'opisany w całości'. The bottom tab bar shows '~/ko', '~/blog', '~/kontakt', '~/o mnie'. Screenshot 2: no button anywhere in the hero. The right side holds a decorative mock terminal ('agent · explore', 'agent · review', 'you · decide').

Suggestion: Add a solid button under the hero text: 'Opisz projekt →' linking to /pl/kontakt/. Add a secondary link, 'Zobacz cennik', next to it. Repeat the button at the end of the page. Keep the blog links, but make them secondary.

high

Navigation is cryptic and the pricing page isn't reachable from the menu

The mobile bottom tabs use file-path labels. 'ko' is an unclear abbreviation for the home page. The menu has no 'Oferta' or 'Cennik' entry. The contact page says a price table and calculators exist, but a first-time visitor can't reach them in one tap from the main navigation. Only Blog, Kontakt and O mnie are visible. The tab bar is clear and thumb-friendly, but the labels cost a stranger a second of decoding.

Evidence: Screenshot 1 bottom bar: '~/ko', '~/blog', '~/kontakt', '~/o mnie'. Screenshot 2 top nav: 'Blog Kontakt O mnie'. Screenshot 3 text: 'Tabela cen podaje widełki, a kalkulatory liczą stronę albo aplikację webową w minutę.'

Suggestion: Rename '~/ko' to 'Start' or 'Home'. Add 'Oferta' or 'Cennik' as a top-level item, and show the calculator links on the home page. Plain labels such as 'Blog' work as well as the terminal style.

medium

Contact is e-mail-based, with the form fields and the real address out of view

The contact page is friendly and sets expectations: a reply within one working day, by e-mail. In the mobile view the actual e-mail address is not visible, and there is no phone number and no tappable mailto above the fold. The form is short, which is good, but the visitor has to scroll to find the message field and the submit button. The form asks for 'na kiedy' (deadline) before the project description. The floating glowing orb at bottom left overlaps the label 'czego potrzebujesz>' and the placeholder text.

Evidence: Screenshot 3: heading 'Porozmawiajmy o Twoim projekcie.' and the text 'Odpisuję w ciągu jednego dnia roboczego, mailem — nie drugim formularzem'. Fields visible: 'imię>', 'email>', 'na kiedy>' (chips 'jak najszybciej', 'w tym miesiącu', 'nie pali się', 'mam termin'), 'czego potrzebujesz>'. The orb overlaps the 'czego potrzebujesz' label. The page data reports 3 form fields, 1 mailto and 0 tel links.

Suggestion: Show the address as a tappable 'mailto:' link right under the intro. Add a phone or messenger link if the owner takes calls. Move the project description above the deadline chips. Move or shrink the floating orb so it doesn't cover the form. Add a one-line confirmation of what happens after sending.

medium

Little proof of work for a stranger: no portfolio, clients or prices on the home page

Trust rests on text only. Client names are withheld for NDA reasons, and the one case study is reachable only through a link to the blog. There is no photo of the owner, no testimonial and no 'from' price on the home page. The mock terminal on desktop is decoration, not proof. The About page does give useful facts: 'dziesięć lat', Kraków, Polish and English, B2B invoice.

Evidence: Screenshots 1 and 2: the only trust signals are 'full-stack developer. 10 lat w komercji, nazwy klientów zostają za NDA'. Screenshot 5 shows no photo, only text paragraphs. Screenshot 4 shows the blog uses a generated-looking hero image.

Suggestion: Add a block on the home page with 2–3 concrete results (for example 'strona w X dni', or the migration case study with numbers), a 'od … zł' price hint with a link to the price table, and a real photo of Kamil on the About page. Quote any client who allows it, even anonymously ('właściciel gabinetu, Kraków').

medium

Dim grey monospace text and the floating orb hurt mobile readability

Long runs of text are grey on a near-black background. On the home page the monospace intro is clearly dimmer than the headline. On the Blog and Contact pages the intro paragraphs are also muted grey. This is tiring to read on a phone in daylight. The floating glowing orb sits in the bottom-left corner on every mobile screenshot and covers text in the lower-left. It overlaps body words on Home ('tylko inne') and About ('prowadzisz').

Evidence: Screenshot 1: the intro 'Kamil Obstawski, full-stack developer. 10 lat w komercji…' is dim grey monospace. The orb overlaps 'tylko inne' in the paragraph. Screenshot 5: the orb overlaps 'prowadzisz firmę'. Screenshot 4: the intro 'Trzy serie i case studies…' is low-contrast grey.

Suggestion: Raise the contrast of secondary text to roughly #c8c4bb or lighter and use the proportional font for paragraphs. Make the orb non-overlapping: smaller, semi-transparent, or hidden on mobile. Keep line length as it is.

low

The blog opens with one long case study but doesn't lead to an offer

The blog intro is written for programmers ('LLM-as-a-Judge', 'migracja legacy'). The first post is part 1 of a series, and the page offers 'curl ./blog/feed.xml'. Business visitors get nothing aimed at them: no link to the price table or contact at the top or in the visible part of the list. The article cards look polished, and the date and reading time ('02 Lis 2026 · 8 min czytania') are helpful.

Evidence: Screenshot 4: '$ curl ./blog/feed.xml', '$ series przepiecie', '1 część', hero image, '02 Lis 2026 · 8 min czytania'. No contact or offer link is visible in the content.

Suggestion: Plain-language label for the feed link ('RSS'). Add a short banner or card on the blog page, 'Potrzebujesz podobnego projektu? Opisz go →', pointing to /pl/kontakt/.

What works well – don’t break it

  • Clean, consistent dark design with a thumb-reachable bottom tab bar on mobile and a working language switch (PL/EN) and light/dark toggle.
  • The contact page is friendly and low-friction: 3 short fields, a clear promise of a reply within one working day, and deadline chips that are easy to tap. The About page states plainly who, where, languages and how billing works.
